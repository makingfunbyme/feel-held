# Candidates

## 1. The apron pocket (selected)

> The apron hangs by the kitchen door. In its pocket is a wooden clothespin, left there when the washing came in. All evening the cloth keeps that small shape.

The finished chore falls away, leaving the shape of one small thing held all evening.

## 2. The low window

> The rain has stopped. A drop hangs from the bottom of the window frame. Through it, a blade of grass fills the sky.

The eye settles on a single drop, and a small change of scale gives it somewhere to stay.

## 3. The saucer

> A little tea has spilled into the saucer. When the cup is lifted, a brown crescent stays behind. The light from the window lies in it.

The movement of the cup ends in a still crescent of light.

## 4. The porch step

> A cat sleeps on the porch step with one paw between the boards. The wood is warm on both sides of it.

The warmth on either side makes the narrow gap feel like a place made for the paw.

## 5. The oars

> The oars lie across the seats. Under the dock, the water lifts the empty boat just enough to darken a new line on the wood.

A thin wet line gives the eye a quiet measure of the boat's movement.

## Round 2

### 1. The ridge in the cloth (selected)

> The apron hangs by the kitchen door. A wooden clothespin lies sideways in the pocket. The light is low enough to show the ridge it makes in the cloth.

Three sentences draw the eye from the door to the pocket to the small ridge in the evening light.

### 2. The folded sheet

> A blue sheet lies folded on the chair. Along one edge, the folds are a little out of line, each a thin blue step.

The uneven folds give the eye a small place to linger and bring out the softness of the sheet.

### 3. The covered bowl

> The dough has risen to the rim of the bowl. In the middle, it lifts the towel just clear of the rim. The corners still hang straight down.

The lift is small enough that the towel stays still at the corners.
